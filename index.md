# Rafael (Rafa) Lopes

I am a computational epidemiologist with a background in physics. I build statistical and mathematical models for incomplete, delayed, and heavy-tailed surveillance data, with a focus on climate-sensitive and mosquito-borne diseases.

I am a Postdoctoral Associate in the Department of Epidemiology of Microbial Diseases and the Public Health Modeling Unit at the [Yale School of Public Health](https://publichealth.yale.edu/), working with the [Grubaugh Lab](https://grubaughlab.com/team/rafael-lopes/) and the [Carlson Lab](https://www.carlsonlab.bio/).

**[Download my CV (PDF, updated October 2026)]({{ "/assets/cv/Rafael_Lopes_CV.pdf" | relative_url }})**

## Research interests

- **Climate and arbovirus epidemics:** attributing how temperature and precipitation shape dengue, chikungunya, and Zika risk, using time-series and extreme-value methods.
- **Nowcasting and forecasting:** correcting for reporting delays in surveillance data and building early-warning systems for outbreak detection.
- **Pathogen genomics and epidemiology:** combining genomic data with infection estimates to track variant replacement, introductions, and the global spread of dengue.
- **Statistical and mathematical epidemiology:** Bayesian hierarchical and mechanistic models of transmission, immunity, and disease burden.

## Education

## Earlier work

Before Yale, I was a researcher at the All for Health Institute in São Paulo, where I built nowcasting and machine learning models for early warning of dengue outbreaks. During the COVID-19 pandemic, my PhD advisor, collaborators, and I founded [Observatório COVID-19 BR](https://covid19br.github.io), an open platform for scientific analysis of the pandemic in Brazil. It is also on [Twitter](https://twitter.com/obscovid19br), [Instagram](https://instagram.com/obscovid19br), and [Facebook](https://facebook.com/obscovid19br).
